## Why we validate at the boundary

This library validates all input at its public boundary — the functions and constructors callers touch directly — rather than scattering checks deeper in the call graph. Once a value passes the boundary, every internal function can trust its shape and simply do its job, without repeating defensive checks or silently tolerating malformed state.

This buys us a few concrete things:

- **Fail fast, fail clearly.** Bad input is rejected the moment it enters the system, with an error that points at the actual cause, instead of surfacing later as a confusing failure deep in internal logic.
- **Simpler internals.** Core logic is written for the valid case only, keeping it shorter, easier to test, and easier to reason about.
- **A stable contract.** Internals can change implementation details freely without renegotiating what "valid input" means, since that contract lives in one place.

In short: validate once, trust everywhere after.
