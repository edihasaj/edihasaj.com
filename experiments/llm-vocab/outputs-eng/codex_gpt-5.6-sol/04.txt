## Background Email Delivery

Email sending should run as a background job rather than inside the request that triggers it. The application will validate the request, persist an email job, and immediately return success. A worker will then claim queued jobs and send messages through the configured email provider.

Each job should include the template, recipient, template data, creation time, attempt count, and a stable idempotency key. The worker should retry transient failures with exponential backoff and jitter, while permanent failures move to a dead-letter queue for inspection. Limit retries and redact message content from logs.

Persist job state so restarts do not lose work. Use atomic claiming or queue-provided visibility timeouts to prevent concurrent processing. Provider timeouts and rate limits must be explicit. Metrics should cover queue depth, delivery latency, success rate, retries, and terminal failures.

Initially, one worker and a durable queue are sufficient. The design should allow worker concurrency to scale independently as email volume grows.
