I started running because I wanted one part of my day that belonged entirely to me.

At first, it was practical. I sat too much, worked late, and felt the usual fog that comes from spending most of the day indoors and online. Running was the simplest answer. Shoes on, out the door, no booking or equipment needed.

It quickly became more than exercise. A run gives my mind room to settle. Problems that feel tangled at a desk often become clear somewhere between the first difficult kilometre and the walk home. I notice the weather, the streets, and the pace of the city in a way I rarely do otherwise.

I am not chasing perfect times or huge distances. I run because it is honest work. You show up, take the next step, and slowly become capable of more than you expected.
