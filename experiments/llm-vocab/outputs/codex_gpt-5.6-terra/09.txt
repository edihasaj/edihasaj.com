Hi — first jobs teach faster than any course.

Be reliable before trying to be impressive. Show up prepared, meet small commitments, and communicate early when something may slip. Ask questions, but do a little homework first; “I looked at X and Y, and I’m unsure about Z” is a strong question.

Treat feedback as information, not a verdict on you. Write things down: names, acronyms, decisions, and recurring processes. Build relationships beyond your direct manager—people remember thoughtful, low-drama collaborators.

Don’t confuse being busy with creating value. Learn what the team is actually measured on, then make your work easier to review and use. Protect your energy too: sustainable consistency beats heroic late nights.

Finally, be patient with yourself. Everyone feels behind at first; competence is mostly accumulated context. Stay curious, kind, and dependable.
