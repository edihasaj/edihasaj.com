I didn't start running because I loved it. I started because I'd stopped noticing my own body.

For years, my days ran on a loop: laptop, coffee, laptop, couch. I wasn't unhealthy exactly, just absent — carrying myself around like luggage. Then one grey Tuesday I missed a bus, jogged half a block to catch the next one, and arrived gasping, heart pounding, embarrassed. That was the moment. Not a doctor's warning, not a New Year's resolution. Just a hundred metres of pavement telling me the truth.

So I laced up a pair of old trainers and ran to the end of my street. It took about ninety seconds and I hated all of them. But the next morning I did it again, and something quiet shifted.

This blog is about what happened after that — the slow, unglamorous, surprisingly joyful business of becoming someone who runs.
