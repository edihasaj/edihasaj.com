Hi—your first job is a beginning, not a final exam.

Be curious, dependable, and easy to work with. Ask questions early instead of hiding confusion, but first spend a few minutes checking documentation or notes. Write down what you learn; small details quickly become valuable knowledge.

Clarify expectations: what does good work look like, which tasks matter most, and when should you share progress? Give brief updates before anyone needs to chase you. If you make a mistake, acknowledge it promptly, explain how you’ll fix it, and learn from it.

Build relationships beyond your immediate team. Listen carefully, remember names, and notice how decisions actually get made. Avoid gossip and office politics.

You do not need to prove yourself by working constantly. Protect your sleep, take breaks, and learn to set reasonable boundaries. Keep a private record of achievements and positive feedback.

Most importantly, compare yourself with who you were last month—not with experienced colleagues. Competence grows quietly through repetition.
