Hi Edi — every long run begins with one honest step.

I started running because I needed somewhere for my thoughts to go. My days had become crowded with screens, notifications, and half-finished ideas. Running offered the opposite: one road, one rhythm, one simple task—keep moving.

At first, I wasn’t chasing distance or speed. I just wanted to feel less restless. The early runs were awkward and harder than expected, but they gave me something I hadn’t realised I was missing: uninterrupted time with myself. Gradually, the discomfort became familiar. A kilometre turned into three, then five, and consistency began to matter more than motivation.

Running hasn’t solved everything, and that was never the point. It has taught me patience, made difficult days feel lighter, and reminded me that progress is often quiet. I started running to clear my head. I kept running because, somewhere along the way, it changed how I move through life.
