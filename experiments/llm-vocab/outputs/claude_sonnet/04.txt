Here's a blog intro about starting a running journey:

---

I didn't start running because I loved it — I started because I needed to remember what it felt like to finish something. Work had become a blur of half-closed tabs and half-finished thoughts, and somewhere along the way I'd stopped noticing the difference between being busy and being present. A friend mentioned she'd started running just to clear her head, and something about the simplicity of it stuck with me: no screen, no deadline, just one foot in front of the other until you decide to stop.

That first run lasted less than ten minutes and left me gasping on my porch steps, questioning every decision that led me there. But underneath the burning lungs was something I hadn't felt in a while — clarity. This is the story of how that shaky, out-of-breath beginning turned into a habit I never saw coming.
